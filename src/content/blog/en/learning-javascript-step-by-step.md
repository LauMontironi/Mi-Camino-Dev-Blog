## 🧩 Splitting the learning into blocks

So as not to overwhelm myself, I split JS into several mental parts:

1. **Variables and data types** – as if they were Excel cells where you store numbers, text or boolean values.
2. **Operators and basic logic** – addition, subtraction, multiplication, comparison (`>`, `<`, `===`), logical operators (`&&`, `||`) and assignment ones (`+=`, `=`).
3. **Functions and loops** – the part I am struggling with most:

```
* Exercises counting even numbers from 1 to 1000

* Painting things red or green depending on conditions

* Breaking out of loops when a certain condition is met

```

1. **Interacting with the DOM** – making something happen on screen:

```
* Buttons that show messages

* Changing background or text colours

* Updating a shopping cart based on calculations

```

---

## 🔢 Logic and calculations

- Some exercises remind me of **maths**, and at first they seem to have no practical point.
- But now I understand that these calculations are the basis of real applications:
  - A shopping cart
  - Prices with VAT
  - Totals and discounts
- Even if for now you see nothing on screen, you are **learning the logic that later gets applied to the DOM and to user interaction**.

---

## 💻 Mini projects and practice

In the meantime, I keep practising outside the Bootcamp:

- **Frontend Mentor challenges**, which I am now actually publishing, although with embarrassment 😅
- Guided exercises with ChatGPT:
  - Arrays
  - Functions
  - Loops
  - Operators
- Experiments with **Bootstrap and Tailwind** (my goodness, it is hard to understand at first!)

This helps me **connect theory and practice** before the Bootcamp moves faster.

---

## 📝 Organisation and tools

- **Notion** has become my ally: I can paste code, organise notes and plan practice sessions.
- It helps me **keep everything in one place** and revisit concepts when I need them.

---

## ✨ Reflections on the Bootcamp

- Even though the more logical, mathematical part seems complicated to me, I know it is **the foundation of all web development**.
- The DOM interaction part is already clearer and more motivating for me.
- I still feel like I did when I was studying on my own, but now with guidance and classmates.
- In the long run, all of this will be useful for **building real projects** and consolidating skills.

---

## 🚀 Next steps

1. Keep going with the logic and function exercises.
2. Build mini projects to **apply the calculations in practice**.
3. Publish challenges and share results to **measure my progress**.
4. Get ready for the more advanced part of the Bootcamp and keep connecting theory with practice.
