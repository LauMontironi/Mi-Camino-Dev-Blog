## 😵‍💫 Guilt, doubts and an emotional whirlwind

Until almost the second year of my second daughter's life, I was still working… or at least trying to.

Carrying the guilt around, and with a constant feeling that **I was not doing anything well**.

On top of that there was something deeper: an enormous sense of depersonalisation. I asked myself over and over:

> Why do I do what I do?
>
> Does this make me happy?
>
> Am I growing professionally?
>
> Can you grow while being a mother? While being a present mother?

It was driving me mad.

## 🤝 A hard decision, but a necessary one

We decided — my husband and I — that I would not work until our youngest started P1. At that point, **we could afford it**, and it was the best thing for our family.

I never had, and still do not have, a nanny or outside help at home, apart from my parents.

And anyone with three children will know what it means to be "alone" at home with that picture.

> An important detail I forgot to mention:
>
> We are a family that, thank God, heaven or the universe (or whatever each person believes in), **stays together** and **moves as one block**.
>
> A month after my husband and I came to Barcelona, my sister came too.
>
> Three years later my parents and my brother arrived for good.

## 🧨 Years "not working", but with my mind at its limit

During those years when I was not in paid work, I was looking after my 4- and 2-year-old daughters… and a newborn.

And that "not working" **sank me mentally**.

My mind could not allow itself to not produce, to not contribute. I went through what I now believe was **undiagnosed depression**, without medication. But I knew something was not right.

My self-esteem, what little was left of it, **disappeared completely**.

## 🤔 Wanting to go back… but how?

Little by little, I wanted to start feeling better. I set myself a clear goal:

> "When my son starts P1, I am going to spend September to December getting back some of my freedom.
>
> At least have the mornings for me, to do something for me. For my mental health."

("Freedom" in quotes, because the mental load and the housework never
disappear… but at least I had a bit more space.)

### 🔍 The job hunt begins (spoiler: it was a nightmare)

In January I started looking for work.

With uncertainty. With no confidence. Carrying the weight of two years
without working and a CV that largely embarrassed me, because I felt stuck
and worthless.

And yes, I did a lot of interviews. And yes, **I was rejected for being a mother**.

Lines like:

- "And how will you manage with three children?"
- "Do you have someone to help you?"
- "The hours at closing time are not flexible"
- "Reduced hours are not possible"

And an endless list of things that prove that **work-life balance does not exist**.

That **equality between men and women is not real**.

(Maybe what damages us most as women is pretending that it is. But that is a whole other article.)

## 😞 A job with no responsibility… and no motivation

I ended up "forced" to accept a **part-time, badly paid** job, with zero chance of growth.

I say "forced" because:

- I did not trust myself.
- I felt **I was not good enough for anything**.
- And when I did manage to get interviews with better conditions, they rejected me.
  (Because I could not offer **total availability** to be a slave to the job.)

### ⏪ I went backwards… a lot

At 37, I found myself working **in worse conditions than when I was an intern at 23**.

Worse salary, smaller role, less growth.

And once again, my head would not stop:

> Is this the profession for me?
>
> Is this what I want?
>
> How do I reconcile my reality as a present mother with a career that motivates me?
>
> How do I work at something I enjoy, that pays me well, and that lets me be there for and because of my children?

It seemed impossible.

The jobs I could get were more of the same:

Badly paid, no flexibility, no chance to grow.

## 💡 What if I change profession?

I had been thinking about a change for a while. But something always stopped me:

- "I am too old to start from scratch."
- "I have no experience."
- "Being a junior again, starting from the bottom again…"
- "What for? … when what I need is a better paid job and that is it"

And on top of that, I could not find a profession that **really fitted me**,
and every time I faced the job hunt my mind would not stop punishing me and
thinking over and over that I was worth nothing and that was why I could
not get better conditions. I thought maybe I really was meant to spend my
whole life in a badly paid part-time admin role, and I blamed myself a lot
too for not being "happy" with a reality that many people would want.

## 💻 The click: code

One day something in my head clicked.

I said:

> "I have to study code.
>
> I need to bring myself up to date.
>
> I need to stop being a digital novice.
>
> I want to see if I like it. I want to understand."

I started by watching **free videos on YouTube**. They interested me, but I did not understand a thing.

Second attempt: I bought a course on Udemy for €14.

I did not finish that one either. It frustrated me not to have a plan, a guide, a clear goal.

### 📚 So… I started looking at bootcamps.

I asked myself:

- Are they necessary?
- Are they a good option?

In the meantime, I kept watching videos.

I did not want to lose the momentum.

I kept testing the water:

> "Is this the path?"
>
> "Could I really enjoy this?"

_(and that is where another chapter starts)_
